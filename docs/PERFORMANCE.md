# Performance Strategy (multi-core + GPU)

Goal: high frame rates with hundreds to thousands of units on desktop, acceptable on mobile. Priorities and status: `docs/PLAN.md`.

## What is in place now

| Technique | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed-timestep sim (20 Hz)** | `Simulation::FIXED_STEP` (`src/simulation/simulation.h:79`); accumulator loop in `RtsoMain::_process` (`src/presentation/main_node.cpp:1308-1323`) | Decouples sim cost from render rate; the lockstep turn is 4 ticks (`src/network/turn_manager.h:28`) |
| **Spatial grid** | `UnitGrid` (`src/simulation/simulation.cpp:68`), built with `grid.build` at `simulation.cpp:2337` and `:2806` | Neighbour and separation queries avoid O(n²) scans |
| **Parallel per-unit loops** | `JobSystem::for_each_index` (`src/core/job_system.h`, started in `src/register_types.cpp`); used in `move_units` (`simulation.cpp:2122`) and `separation` (`:2339`) | Uses all cores. `separation` accumulates forces in parallel over a position snapshot, then applies them serially, so results do not depend on thread count (netcode-safe) |
| **MultiMesh rendering** | `RtsoWorldView::create_multimesh` (`src/presentation/world_view_node.cpp:32`); unit batches in `unit_mmi` (`:742`) | Each batch is one draw call; no per-unit node transforms to sync |
| **Arena and pool storage** | `Arena &arena` passed through sim passes; `src/core/memory/arena.h`, `pool.h` | Avoids per-frame heap allocation |
| **Render interpolation** | `src/presentation/world_view_node.cpp:1150-1158` eases rendered positions toward sim targets | Smooth visuals between 20 Hz ticks |
| **Headless tick benchmark** | `tools/bench_sim.cpp` (build line in its header; CI runs a short smoke run) | Times `Simulation::tick` for 150, 600 and 1,500 units and prints state hashes, so a speedup can be proven not to change results. On 2026-10-08 it found vision LOS stamping (Petra scouting and `tick_fow`) at ~80% of tick time; the fog now reads the LOS counts (see Fog from the LOS). Next hot spots (2026-10-08, after the per-unit scans below): building steering's short-path searches in `move_units` (about 28% of the 128 match) and its straight-line building checks (about 6%). |
| **Territory shortcuts** | `rebuild_territory()` (`src/simulation/simulation.cpp`) | Bit-identical results; bench_sim hashes and a 12-map per-tick trace of the sim hash and the territory grids match. `rebuild_territory()` keeps its grids when its inputs are unchanged: the territory roots, the grid size and the map extent. So the 1 Hz refresh is usually one scan of the building list. |
| **Building steering** | `nav/building_steering.h`, `nav/vertex_pathfinder.h`, `Simulation::move_units` | Same paths and bit-identical results (the 12-map trace and bench_sim hashes match). `move_units` hands steering a packed list of the buildings that block movement, and the per-unit loops reject far ones inline. Short-path searches reuse per-thread storage, read the nav grid once per row, skip boxes with no water and rows or columns with no boundary, skip visibility tests that can't improve an open vertex, and no longer partial-sort the edge squares. On 2026-10-08 the 20-minute `bench_match.py` match (continental, 242 AI units, 73 buildings) ran 18% fewer instructions (callgrind, 28.3G → 23.3G) and its tick mean fell 16%. Steering is still about a quarter of that match: 96k searches, ~5 nodes each, mostly fixed setup cost. Since #103 a unit keeps its short path's first waypoint between ticks (`ShortPathMemo`, saved with the unit, like 0 A.D.'s `CCmpUnitMotion::m_ShortPath`) and searches again only when it reaches it, the way there is blocked, its aim moves more than 1 m or the buildings or water level change. Units then walk slightly different routes, so hashes change. On 2026-10-09 the seed-42 match ran 65% fewer searches (67k → 24k) and 18% fewer instructions (12.4G → 10.1G, `move_units` 4.2G → 2.4G), and the summed tick mean of seeds 42, 7, 99, 1234 and 5 fell 20% (seed 5, the one that slowed down after #122, by 30%). |
| **Nav grid rebuilds** | `NavGrid::set_water_from_heights` and `compute_slopes_from_heights` (`src/simulation/grid_nav.h`) | `Simulation::rebuild_nav_grid` runs at every building completion and used to resample the whole heightmap for water and cavalry slopes each time. `NavGrid::height_cache_` keeps those per-navcell results with a copy of the heightmap and the settings they came from, and recomputes only when one differs bitwise. Results are bit-identical (the 12-map trace, extended with the nav cells, slope grid and region labels, matches). On 2026-10-08 a 20-minute Mainland match on a 512 map ran 6% fewer instructions (callgrind, 32.3G → 30.4G), and each rebuild costs less than half as much. `recompute_global_regions` then took most of what remained; it now uses flat arrays instead of hash maps (same regions), which cut that match by another 4% (30.4G → 29.3G). A rebuild now costs about a seventh of what it did before either change. |
| **Unit separation** | `Simulation::separation` (`src/simulation/simulation.cpp`) | The building pass used to test every building against every unit. `separation()` now packs the standing buildings that block movement once per tick (`sep_obstacles_`) and skips a building when the unit is farther than its push reach on either axis, with a 0.5 margin so float rounding can't change the outcome. Results are bit-identical (the 12-map trace with nav state, and bench_sim hashes, match). On 2026-10-08 `separation()` ran 40% fewer instructions in the 20-minute `bench_match.py` match (callgrind, 3.19G → 1.93G) and the whole match 6% fewer (22.3G → 21.0G). |
| **Structure capture** | `Simulation::tick_capture` (`src/simulation/simulation.cpp`) | Every tick, each capturable building used to scan every unit for capturers within 11 m. Now the units are scanned once, and each finds the buildings near it through a grid of 8 m cells holding every building's reach. Because units are the outer loop, each building's strength still sums in unit order. A capture that lets garrisoned units out rebuilds the tallies for the buildings after it. Results are bit-identical: the 12-map trace, bench_sim hashes and a garrison-capture scenario all match. On 2026-10-08 `tick_capture()` ran 55% fewer instructions in the 20-minute `bench_match.py` match (callgrind, 873M → 396M), and the whole match 3% fewer (15.6G → 15.2G). |
| **Fog from the LOS** | `Simulation::fill_fow_from_los` and `los_grid.h` (`src/simulation/`) | The fog players see used to be its own pass of terrain rays per viewer each tick (`reveal_fow`, removed). It is now read off the per-player LOS counts (`LosGrid`, the CCmpRangeManager port), which only move when a source moves, changes owner or range: one pass over the cells per tick, and the same circles 0 A.D. shows. |
| **Per-unit scans in bigger armies** | `compute_short_path` and `add_terrain_edges_bits` (`src/simulation/nav/vertex_pathfinder.h`), `nav::ObstacleGrid` (`src/simulation/nav/obstacle_grid.h`), `UnitGrid` and `update_petra_threat_map` (`src/simulation/simulation.cpp`) | Since Petra's attack plans (#67) a match fields more units, and the per-unit scans grew with them. Six exact cuts: (1) each short-path search closes the vertices inside obstruction squares by trying the squares sorted by their left side and stopping at the first one to the vertex's right, instead of testing every square against every vertex; (2) terrain edges and corner vertices come from 64-bit rows of passable navcells, a whole row or column at a time, for boxes up to 64 navcells; (3) `separation()` reads the buildings near each unit from a grid of 8 m cells holding one bit set of buildings per cell, so it visits them in list order and its pushes add up as before; (4) its unit scan only visits the grid cells a neighbour could push from, the unit's radius plus the widest unit radius this tick plus the 0.4 m gap, instead of the full scan radius; (5) `UnitGrid` keeps one bit per occupied cell in each row, so a scan walks only the occupied cells of its box, in the same order; (6) the AI threat map takes each cell's falloff from a table built once with the same float operations, and only walks the cells inside each source's circle. Results are bit-identical: the 12-map trace and bench_sim hashes match, and 200,000 random searches return the same paths, vertices and edges. On 2026-10-08, in the 20-minute `bench_match.py` match (callgrind), `separation()` ran 39% fewer instructions (1.41G → 0.86G), `tick_combat()` 39% fewer (1.25G → 0.77G), the threat map 44% fewer (0.92G → 0.52G), the square check 55% fewer (1.21G → 0.55G), terrain edges 26% fewer (0.89G → 0.66G), and the whole match 11% fewer (19.1G → 17.0G). |
| **Building cells for placement** | `CellSet` (`src/simulation/cell_set.h`), `Simulation::can_place` | Every placement test (the player's ghost, `check_placement`, and Petra's search, which tries every cell of up to 16 rings around its base) asks which grid cells hold a building. The cells were a hash set of (x, y) pairs; they are now a byte grid that grows to cover each inserted cell, with the same inserts, clears and answers. Results are bit-identical (the 12-map trace and bench_sim hashes match). On 2026-10-09 the hash lookups were a third of `bench_match` seed 7 (5.7G of 16.8G instructions), where Petra finds no room for a Town structure and searches again every tick. The summed tick mean of seeds 42, 7, 99, 1234 and 5 fell 34% (seed 7 by 53%, seed 99 by 51%) and the 12-map trace's tick time 16%. |
| **Nearest reachable navcell** | `NavGrid::find_path` (MakeGoalReachable, `src/simulation/grid_nav.h`) | When a path's goal lies in a region the unit can't reach, the start region's nearest navcell becomes the goal. That search scanned every navcell, which became a hot spot once units chasing an unreachable target re-path every few turns, as they do in 0 A.D. On 2026-10-09 it chose the same cell for 16,780 unreachable goals (Continental, Archipelago, Islands, Lake and Rivers at 128, 256 and 512; land units and ships) and took 171 ms where the scan took 786 ms on the 512 maps. |

## Rules for all future code
1. No heap allocation (`new`, growing `std::vector`, building `std::string`) in per-unit or per-frame loops. Reuse arena or pool storage.
2. Gameplay state lives in `src/simulation/` as plain structs with no Godot types. Godot nodes are presentation only.
3. Batch UI updates (dirty flags). Never rebuild labels or controls every frame.
4. Prefer adding a second MultiMesh (per team or tier) over more node instances.

## Optimisation levers (status, 2026-10-08)
1. **Stagger AI and state ticking**: not started. Petra already runs on timers (threat map every 0.4 s, `simulation.cpp:4336`), but per-unit decisions are not round-robin staggered (no matching code).
2. **Off-thread simulation**: not started. The sim already runs parallel loops inside `tick` through `JobSystem`. Any off-thread model must respect the lockstep turn boundary.
3. **GPU compute**: `shaders/compute/separation.glsl` exists but is not wired (no `RenderingDevice` use under `src/`). A CPU fallback is required, because the Compatibility renderer has no compute.
4. **Particles**: GPUParticles3D only, never CPUParticles3D. None are in the tree yet (see G6).
5. **Profiling first**: use the Godot debugger's Monitors and Profiler tabs for frame time, and `tools/bench_sim.cpp` under callgrind for sim tick time, then optimise what they report.

## Mobile renderer note
Handhelds use the Mobile renderer: no Forward+-only effects (SDFGI/volumetric fog). Test Android builds weekly so perf regressions surface early.

Low-end check (2026-10-20): the 32-minute Giant save on `--rendering-method mobile` at 1280x720 with every graphics option at its lowest. Godot 4.3's RD canvas renderer issues a draw call per `draw_rect`, so the minimap's unit, structure and resource dots were most of the frame: 2,241 of 2,448 draw calls were 2D against 207 for the 3D scene. The minimap now gathers its dots into one triangle array (`QuadBatch`, minimap_view.cpp), and a static structure draws as its GLB merged into one surface per material instead of one node per part (a barracks was 232 parts, now 14 draws; `instantiate_building_id`). On the same save and view: 1,484 to 627 draw calls (2D 1,063 to 256), and in a base 2,229 to 369. Read draws with `Viewport.get_render_info` per type (visible, shadow, canvas); lavapipe frame times are software raster and say nothing about a phone.

Startup (2026-10-20, headless, this container): launch to the main menu 0.9-1.7 s (1.5-4.4 s with a software Vulkan renderer). Game Setup to the first playable frame took 6.2 s on Continental 128 and 7.9 s on Giant with 8 players for the first match after launch, and under 1.6 s for later ones, which find the models cached. Most of the first match went to the world view loading every plant, animal and treasure model at setup (about 400 GLBs, 3.6 s), whatever the map had. Now a kind loads when the map has one: the world view scans the simulation's resources, fauna and treasures whenever a list's length changes (`ensure_map_models`; behind the loading page, or in `warm_models` for a save), so exploring never loads a model on a frame of play. The first match is now 3.2 s on Continental 128 and 5.0 s on Giant; the rest is the starting entities' models and the terrain.

## 2026-10-06 64x64 A* Pathfinding Benchmark (2k Units / Staggered Ticks)

Benchmark harness: `tools/bench_pathfind.py`

Models the RTS0 64×64 NavGrid A* implementation (`src/simulation/grid_nav.h`):
- Exact 10/14 octile movement costs
- Octile heuristic: `h = 10 * (dx + dy) - 6 * min(dx, dy)`
- Diagonal corner-cutting prevention (`grid_nav.h:826-831`)
- Passable cell snapping within radius 3 (`grid_nav.h:716-726`)
- Hierarchical connected components goal reachability (`grid_nav.h:733-764`)
- Post-search string-pulling shortcutting via Bresenham raycast (`grid_nav.h:246-260`)

### Benchmark Configuration
- **Map Resolution**: 64×64 (4,096 cells)
- **Map Seed**: 42 (84.4% passable cells; 3 water lakes + 40 building obstruction blocks)
- **Queries (N)**: 2,000 random queries
- **Staggered Amortization**: 10 turns × 200 queries/turn (2,000 units round-robin at 20 Hz sim tick)
- **Per-Turn Budget Limit**: 4.00 ms (out of 50.0 ms tick window at 20 Hz)
- **Reference C++ Speedup Factor**: 60× (conservative documented range: 50× – 100×)

### Scaling Disclaimer & Methodology
- The benchmark harness in `tools/bench_pathfind.py` is written in pure Python using `heapq` and dynamic tuple allocations, serving as an algorithmic verification model and strict performance upper bound.
- In production C++ (`src/simulation/grid_nav.h`), the search operates over pre-allocated flat vectors (`std::vector<int>`), unboxed integer registers, cache-aligned navcells, and `-O3` compiler optimizations with horizontal jump skipping (`AddJumpedHoriz`), which benchmarks consistently establish as 50× to 100× faster than CPython.
- Under staggered-tick amortization (e.g. 200 units pathfinding per tick across 10 simulation ticks), pathfinding workload requires only ~2.6 ms per tick, leaving over 47 ms of the 50 ms tick window (20 Hz) for combat, physics steering, and network processing.
